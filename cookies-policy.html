The current position

The current ORBIT website application does not deliberately set first-party analytics cookies, advertising cookies, personalisation cookies, tracking pixels, browser local storage or similar optional identifiers. There is no behavioural advertising, cross-site tracking or visitor profiling in the site code.

Because no optional cookies are currently placed by ORBIT, the site does not display a consent banner solely for the purpose of accepting or rejecting analytics or advertising cookies. A banner that asks for consent to technologies that do not exist would be misleading.

This does not mean that a web request is anonymous. The hosting environment and external asset providers may receive ordinary technical request information such as IP address, browser type, referrer and timestamp. That processing is explained in the Privacy Policy and below.

Cookie behaviour can change when hosting, security, analytics, embedded media or marketing tools are added. The live deployment should be re-audited before launch and whenever the technology stack changes.

What cookies and similar technologies are

A cookie is a small text record stored on a browser or device when a website is visited. Cookies can be created by the website being visited (“first-party”) or by another service loaded by that website (“third-party”). A session cookie normally expires when the browser session ends, while a persistent cookie remains until its configured expiry date or manual deletion.

Websites may also use technologies that perform related functions without being traditional cookies. Examples include local storage, session storage, software-development-kit identifiers, tracking pixels, device fingerprinting, cache identifiers and URL parameters. This policy uses “cookies” as a convenient collective term where the same transparency and consent principles apply.

Strictly necessary
Required to deliver a service explicitly requested by the visitor, maintain security, balance traffic or remember a privacy choice. These should be limited to what is genuinely necessary.
Preference
Remember choices such as language, layout or region when the site can function without remembering them.
Analytics
Measure visits, navigation, performance or interaction so a site owner can understand usage.
Advertising
Build or use profiles, measure campaigns, retarget visitors or follow activity across websites or services.

Consent and applicable rules

Under European electronic-communications rules, storing information on or accessing information from a visitor’s terminal equipment generally requires clear information and prior consent unless the technology is strictly necessary to provide a service expressly requested by the visitor.

Where consent is required, it must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. Optional cookies must not be placed before that choice is made. Consent for one purpose must not be bundled with unrelated purposes, and refusing optional cookies must not be made unnecessarily harder than accepting them. Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent.

Data collected through cookies may also be personal data. In that case, the GDPR applies in addition to cookie-specific rules, including requirements for a lawful basis, transparency, data minimisation, security, retention and individual rights.

Official EU guidance on consent-based cookies is available through Your Europe.

Strictly necessary technologies

A production host may use a narrowly scoped mechanism for functions such as traffic routing, denial-of-service protection, form-abuse prevention or preserving a security decision during a session. A technology is not “strictly necessary” merely because it is useful to the website owner; it must be essential to provide the requested function or secure that function.

If such a mechanism is active, it may be used without optional-cookie consent where the applicable exemption is satisfied. It must still be documented, kept for no longer than necessary and protected appropriately. It must not be repurposed for analytics, advertising or cross-site profiling without a separate lawful basis and consent where required.

The contact form uses a hidden field to detect basic automated submissions. This field is part of the submitted form and does not store an identifier on the visitor’s device.

External fonts, icons and third-party requests

Google Fonts

The website currently loads Archivo and IBM Plex Mono using Google Fonts. The browser contacts Google-operated infrastructure to request the stylesheet and font files. The provider can receive standard network information required to return the files, including IP address, browser information, requested resource, referrer and time of request.

UNPKG and Lucide

The Lucide icon library is currently loaded from UNPKG. The browser therefore sends a request to that content-delivery service. ORBIT uses the response to render interface icons and does not instruct the service to build an ORBIT advertising profile.

These external requests are relevant to privacy even where no cookie is written. The providers’ own terms and infrastructure govern their independent processing. ORBIT may reduce external requests by self-hosting these assets in a future production build; if that change is made, this policy will be updated.

If optional technologies are added

If ORBIT later introduces analytics, embedded media, advertising tools, conversion measurement or personalisation that requires consent, those technologies will remain disabled until the visitor makes an affirmative choice.

Before activation, the consent interface will identify the relevant purposes and categories, provide a genuine reject option, avoid pre-selected optional choices and record the preference only as necessary to respect it. Visitors will be able to change or withdraw the choice through an accessible control.

The inventory in this policy will be expanded to state, for each relevant technology:

  • the cookie or storage name;
  • the provider and whether it is first-party or third-party;
  • the precise purpose and category;
  • the information stored or accessed;
  • the lifetime or expiry rule;
  • whether data is combined across services or transferred internationally;
  • how consent can be withdrawn.

Browser and device controls

Most browsers allow you to inspect, block or delete cookies through privacy or site-data settings. You can usually block all cookies, block third-party cookies, clear data for a particular domain or configure deletion when the browser closes.

Blocking all cookies can affect websites that genuinely need a session to provide a requested function. On the current ORBIT site, core content and the enquiry form do not depend on an optional analytics or advertising cookie.

Controls differ by browser and version. Use the browser’s official help documentation rather than relying on instructions that may become outdated. Deleting browser data does not automatically delete personal data already contained in an email enquiry or business record; those requests are handled under the Privacy Policy.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

Some browsers transmit signals such as Do Not Track (DNT) or Global Privacy Control (GPC). There is not one universally applied legal or technical interpretation for every signal and jurisdiction.

The present site does not deploy behavioural advertising or sell/share visitor data for cross-context behavioural advertising, so there is no optional advertising system to disable in response to such a signal. If future functionality creates an applicable obligation, ORBIT will assess recognised browser signals and implement the required response.

Duration, deletion and server logs

A cookie’s duration is separate from server-log retention. Deleting a cookie removes the browser-side value but does not erase a server record that was lawfully created from an earlier request.

The current application has no ORBIT cookie lifetime to list because it does not deliberately create a cookie. Standard hosting and security logs are normally retained for a limited operational period as described in the Privacy Policy. If a production security provider creates a necessary cookie, its exact lifetime must be added to the inventory above.

Technology audits and policy updates

Cookie compliance is an operational process, not a one-time paragraph. ORBIT should review the production website after deployment and whenever scripts, tags, embeds, consent tools, hosting layers or marketing integrations change.

This policy may be revised to reflect the result of those reviews or changes in law and regulatory guidance. The effective date at the top will be updated when a new version is published. If a material change introduces consent-based technology, an appropriate on-site notice and consent control will be implemented before the technology is activated.
