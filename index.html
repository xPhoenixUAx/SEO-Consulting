THE PRACTICE

WHAT WE ACTUALLY DO.

We find what is getting in the way, fix the highest-impact issues and give your team a practical plan for the months ahead.

AUDIT & TECHNICAL SEO

Find what is blocking visibility.

We crawl the site, review how important pages are indexed and trace problems back to their source. You get a short, ranked list of fixes instead of a hundred-page export.

  • We review crawl access, index coverage, templates and site structure. Each finding is ranked by likely impact, urgency and the effort needed to fix it.

  • We rewrite or restructure the elements that help a page explain itself: titles, headings, introductions and supporting sections. The language still sounds like your brand, not an SEO template.

  • We find orphaned pages and weak navigation paths, then show where internal links can help visitors and search engines reach the pages that matter most.

  • We check canonicals, robots rules, sitemaps, redirects, duplicate URLs and response codes, then write implementation notes your developers can use without translating an SEO report first.

CONTENT STRATEGY

Turn search demand into useful pages.

We start with the questions customers ask, match them to business priorities and decide which pages deserve to be created, improved or removed.

  • We group searches by the problem behind them and the decision someone is trying to make. That shows which page should answer each need and where the site currently has gaps or overlap.

  • We organize service pages, guides and supporting resources so related subjects sit together and every important page has a clear place in the site.

  • Each brief explains who the page is for, the questions it must answer, the evidence it needs and where it should lead next. Writers get a useful brief, not a script.

  • We improve existing copy, metadata, headings, images and internal links. The aim is a better page for the reader, not a higher keyword count.

AUTHORITY & GROWTH

Build reasons to be trusted.

Good links usually follow good work. We help you identify what is worth citing, who would genuinely care about it and how to earn recognition without buying volume.

  • We identify research, tools, expert commentary and partnerships that could earn relevant mentions. We care more about where a link appears and why than the size of a link package.

  • We build supporting content around the subjects your business genuinely knows. The result is a connected body of expertise, not a collection of articles written around isolated keywords.

  • Reviews compare the work completed with changes in indexation, relevant rankings, traffic and enquiries. We separate a useful trend from the normal week-to-week noise.

  • Every review ends with a short list of next actions and a named owner. Your team knows what to do next and why it is worth doing.

AN HONEST EXPECTATION

SEO moves in months, not moments.

A reliable trend usually takes three to six months to appear while search engines crawl, reassess and compare. Anyone guaranteeing page one in 30 days is overselling.

FIELD NOTES

START WITH THE BASICS.

Three notes for teams deciding what to fix, what to publish and which SEO advice is worth ignoring.

TECHNICAL / 06 MINWhat an SEO audit should help you decide

Most crawl tools can produce thousands of warnings. That does not mean thousands of things need fixing. A good audit helps the team decide what deserves attention now and what can safely wait.

For each priority, ask four questions: which pages are affected, what is the likely consequence, how difficult is the fix and who can do it? The first five actions should be obvious.

EXPLORE THE RELATED SERVICE
CONTENT / 07 MINWhy more content is often the wrong first move

Publishing every week does not help if the site keeps answering the same question in slightly different ways. Sometimes the better move is to improve, merge or remove an existing page.

Before commissioning an article, decide who needs it, which question it will answer and what your business can add that is not already on the first page of results.

EXPLORE THE RELATED SERVICE
AUTHORITY / 05 MINA credible link has context before it has a metric

A link is valuable when it appears on a relevant page, in front of the right audience and for a credible reason. A high metric on its own tells you very little.

Start by creating something worth referring to: useful research, a practical tool, a strong example or genuine expert commentary. Outreach is much easier when there is a real reason to reply.
